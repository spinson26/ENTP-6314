# Complaint Themes & What Humans Are Paid to Negotiate Deals

**Prepared:** September 25, 2026
**Scope:** Complaints from the last 18 months (since March 25, 2025) about products that do part of what this app would do, plus what companies pay humans to negotiate sports deals.

> **Access note.** Reddit is blocked. **G2 and Capterra do not list any of these products** — they cover business software, and every relevant product here is a game, a fan tool or a consumer app. Evidence is **1,921 negative Steam reviews** (six PC sports-management games) and **213 critical App Store reviews** (ten mobile GM, agent-sim and AI-trainer apps), all posted since March 25, 2025. Complaints are paraphrased with app, rating, date and reviewer handle; the originals are in [steam-negative-reviews.json](steam-negative-reviews.json) and [appstore-reviews.json](appstore-reviews.json).

### Theme 1 — Monetization changes — 61/213 mobile reviews (29%) — **PRICING problem**

Franchise Hockey: Pro GM changed its monetization in late summer 2026 and its review page collapsed. Roughly a dozen one-star reviews in the first ten days of September 2026 alone.

1. **Franchise Hockey: Pro GM, 1★, Sept 10 2026 (*Easygoing 10*)** — the latest update makes you watch ads for everything; after years of playing they're considering deleting it, and note the developer's reply admitted things that were free are now behind paywalls or ad walls. ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1327981700?see-all=reviews))
2. **Franchise Hockey: Pro GM, 1★, Sept 5 2026 (*Dherman718*)** — minute-long ads now required for training and packs that used to be instant, on top of a $4 subscription.
3. **FM26 Mobile, 1★, Aug 31 2026 (*wkjshdhebwksuabdbbfdkh*)** — requires a ~$20/month Netflix subscription to play; listing it as "free" is called a lie. ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6446123740?see-all=reviews))

> **Also:** Ultimate Pro Basketball GM, 1★, Jul 28 2026 (*Lilkd1233*) — bought the premium edition and still **"can't even make trades without TP"** (the in-game currency). **That is the negotiation itself being paywalled.**

### Theme 2 — Trade and negotiation AI is unrealistic — 74/213 mobile (35%), and the top franchise complaint on Steam — **PRODUCT problem**

1. **Franchise Hockey Manager 12, Jun 1 2026, 187h, +14 (Steam)** — the AI "makes the dumbest trades in history and will also offer you pennies on the dollar"; a point-per-game star drew a **7th-round pick**. ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3739670/negativereviews/))

3. **Ultimate Pro Basketball GM, 1★, Mar 21 2026 (*TheMarshEffect*)** — "The trades are super one sided… A great concept to bring to phones just a horrible execution." ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1552760865?see-all=reviews))

> **Also:** MockOut (NFL draft sim), 3★, Apr 1 2025 (*MoMentum 100*) — wants the app to **"use the NFL trade charts to validate trades"** rather than produce implausible ones. **That reviewer is asking for exactly the leverage model your app proposes.**

### Theme 3 — You can't actually propose a deal — **PRODUCT problem**

The single most on-point theme for this project: the products don't let the user *make an offer*.

1. **Front Office Football Nine, Dec 15 2025, +8 (Steam)** — "**You are unable to actually create your own trades** — you simply select a team and a draft pick/player, then hit [a button]." Teams with 100% interest and the assets still can't complete the deal. ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2633170/negativereviews/))
2. **MockOut, 2★, Apr 14 2025 (*BrockSlibun*)** — "This app was outstanding until suddenly I **can't force trades** in my mock drafts anymore… with forced trades it is a five star app."
3. **Madden NFL 26, Apr 21 2026, 392h, +52 (Steam)** — **GM mode was removed after purchase**: "They took away GM Mode... AFTER I PAID $70 FOR THIS GAME."

### Theme 5 — The cap and the rules break — **PRODUCT problem**

1. **Football Manager 26, Sept 13 2026, 154h (Steam)** — an MLS **salary-cap bug during contract negotiations**: the Budget Adjustments screen shows incorrect figures and fails to recognise available GAM/TAM, making the negotiation unusable.
2. **Pocket GM: Basketball Sim, 2★, Jan 15 2026 (*Bdon022*)** — 10–15 seasons in, salaries max out every team's cap, so top free agents go unsigned and **trades become near impossible**. ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6476059372?see-all=reviews))
3. **Ultimate Pro Basketball GM, 1★, Mar 21 2026 (*TheMarshEffect*)** — "How do I compete with teams who have over 200mil in players while I'm limited to 130m cap space?"

**If the cap maths is wrong, the practice is worthless — you are teaching leverage against a broken rulebook.**

### Theme 6 — AI scoring can't be trusted — **PRODUCT problem, and the one to worry about**

Only two reviews exist for the AI-trainer analog, but one is devastating:

1. **CoachCraft: AI Sales Trainer, 1★, Dec 11 2025 (*JKRNH*)** — "Did one lesson. **Did a poor job on purpose. It said I did great.**" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6740181929?see-all=reviews))
2. **CoachCraft, 1★, Dec 5 2025 (*CarloskiDaGreat*)** — the app never got past the first screen after signup.

**An AI trainer that praises a deliberately bad performance is worse than no trainer** — and if your product's value is "prove the skill," that single failure mode destroys the entire proposition.

## What companies pay humans to negotiate sports deals

### The commission structure — what the work is actually worth

| League | Agent commission on playing contracts | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **NFL** | **Capped at 3%** by the NFLPA | [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/how-much-do-nfl-agents-make/) |
| **NBA** | **Capped at 4%** by the NBPA; 3% common for established players | [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/how-much-do-nfl-agents-make/) |
| **MLB** | **No cap**; 4–5% is the industry standard | [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/how-much-do-nfl-agents-make/) |
| **Endorsements (all sports)** | **10–20%** — unregulated, because the contract isn't with a team | [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/how-much-do-nfl-agents-make/) |

**Do the arithmetic:** a 3% commission on a single $5M NFL contract is **$150,000** for one negotiation. **This is a skill with enormous variance — nearly worthless at the bottom and extremely valuable at the top.**

### What this says about who could pay for the product

**The people who most need the practice are the ones with the least money.** The average sports agent earns **$49,833** — below the US median household income — while the deal-making skill itself pays $150,000 on one good contract. The money is concentrated in a few hundred people at the top.

Three consequences:

1. **Students can't pay much**, and they're the core user in Section 1's sizing.
2. **The institutional buyer is the realistic one** — the 441 programs teaching sport management, who buy curriculum, not the individual.
3. **The high-value buyer is the law-school/agency track** — sports lawyers at $100K+ and agencies whose associates negotiate real contracts. **That is a smaller, richer audience than "aspiring sports business students," and nothing in this research says they want a simulator.** `[UNVERIFIED — no evidence either way.]`
