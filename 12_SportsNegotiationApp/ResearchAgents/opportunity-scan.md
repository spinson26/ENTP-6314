# Opportunity Scan — Sports Negotiation Practice App

**Prepared:** September 25, 2026
**Analyst role:** Market research analyst
**Problem space:** People who want to work in sports business have no way to practice or prove the one skill that gets someone hired — reading leverage and structuring a deal. Franchise-mode games hide negotiation behind a probability check, fantasy has no counterparty, and cap sites are static calculators. Meanwhile the industry is oversaturated and the internships that would build the judgment go to a small, connected pool.

> **Access note.** Reddit is blocked to this tool. G2 and Capterra list business software, not games or fan tools. **The usable complaint source here is Steam**, whose public review API returns full text, dates, playtime and vote counts — I pulled **1,921 negative reviews posted since March 2025** across six sports-management games. See **Section 5** for everything unverified.

**Put those together:** roughly **17,000 new graduates a year** chase jobs in a world with **154 major-league front offices** and **12,870 agents nationally across all of entertainment and sport**. The premise that the funnel is brutal is correct and sourced.

### 1c. The adjacent buyers the premise doesn't mention

- **441 institutions** teaching this subject, who buy curriculum and simulation tools.
- **~68,000 D-I athletics staff** who need to understand revenue-share and NIL contracts after the House settlement.
- **554,298 NCAA athletes** competed in 2024–25, an all-time high ([NCAA](https://www.ncaa.org/news/2025/9/15/media-center-a-record-number-of-ncaa-student-athletes-participated-in-2024-25.aspx)) — every one of them is now a potential party to a deal, which is new.

### 1d. Evidence the practice gap is real

The clearest proof that this skill has no practice venue is that **the only serious one is a law-school moot court**: the **Tulane International Baseball Arbitration Competition** simulates MLB salary arbitration, drawing **40 teams from law schools across the US and Canada**, judged by "more than a dozen top MLB executives, agents, and sportswriters" ([Tulane Law](https://law.tulane.edu/baseball-arbitration-competition-brings-heavy-hitters-tulane)).

**Forty teams a year, for law students only, once a year.** That is the entire formal practice infrastructure I could find for deal-making in sport.

### The frequency finding — and it cuts against the premise

| Slice of the 1,921 negative reviews | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Mention franchise, GM mode, contracts, trades or the cap at all | **152** | **7.9%** |
| Mention negotiation, trade logic, free agency or holdouts specifically | **14** | **0.7%** |

**Nine out of ten unhappy buyers are complaining about something else** — bugs, price, roster updates, on-field gameplay, monetization. **The people who do raise negotiation are heavy users** (the trade-AI complaints come from accounts with 115, 187, 309, 426 and 1,898 hours), which suggests deep engagement rather than broad demand.

**Read plainly: the evidence shows an intense minority, not a market screaming for this.** That is the single most important finding in this scan and it should shape whatever gets built next.

## 4. Why might this be newly solvable with AI in 2026?

**(a) A counterparty that argues back is now buildable — and the adjacent category is funded.**
AI role-play training is a real, funded product category: **Second Nature raised a $22M Series B in October 2025** ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/second-nature-secures-22m-series-120000783.html)), and **Hyperbound raised a $15M Series A in September 2025**, running "AI personas trained on 2M+ hours of real B2B call data" with multiparty deal scenarios ([Hyperbound](https://www.hyperbound.ai/)). **Somebody has already proven people will train against an AI counterparty — in sales, not sport.**

**(b) LLMs can now hold a multi-turn bargain, and that is measurable.**
Negotiation benchmarks exist and are active: **NegotiationArena** and **BargainArena** evaluate multi-turn, multi-agent bargaining with private reservation values, and 2025–26 papers measure model behaviour directly — Claude-3 skews aggressive, GPT-4 splits fairly, Llama-3 strikes the most effective bargains. ([Measuring Bargaining Abilities of LLMs](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15813v3); [Diagnosing LLM Negotiation Agents Beyond Deal Rate](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13909))

**(c) The rulebook and the market data are public.** Spotrac and Over The Cap publish contract structures and cap positions; the league CBAs are public documents. **A simulator's ground truth doesn't have to be licensed** — unlike the roster and likeness rights that constrain the games.

### Where AI does not solve this

- **Strategic depth is exactly the measured weakness.** A 2026 paper is titled "**Counterparty Modeling is Not Strategy: The Limits of LLM Negotiators**," and the literature reports "underdeveloped Theory-of-Mind, restricted strategic adaptability, and often superficial reasoning," with models faltering "on consistency, legitimacy, and commitment when stakes rise" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.16575)). An opponent that can be talked into a bad deal teaches the wrong lesson.
- **There is no ground truth for "a good deal."** Sales role-play scores against a known outcome; a contract negotiation has no answer key. `[Inference — I found no scoring rubric or benchmark for evaluating a simulated sports deal.]`
- **A score in an app is not a hiring signal** unless employers accept it, and nothing in this research shows any front office or agency recognising a simulator credential. `[UNVERIFIED — absence of evidence.]`
- **The games' problem is not model quality.** Front Office Football's trade system fails because it won't let the user propose a deal at all; Football Manager's cap maths was wrong in MLS. Those are design and engineering failures, not missing AI.
