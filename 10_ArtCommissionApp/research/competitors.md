## Per-product notes

### 1. Saatchi Art — the incumbent, and it just got more expensive
The largest curated online marketplace for original art, with the "View in a Room" feature your spec describes. **It raised its artist commission from 35% to 40% in 2026** — which lands squarely inside the 30–50% gallery range your premise attacks. **The online alternative to the gallery now charges gallery rates.** `[The rate change is reported consistently across secondary sources; Saatchi's own help page blocks this tool.]`

### 2. Artfinder — the closest thing to your commission flow, and explicitly anti-AI
It already runs a **"Commission an Artwork"** request flow, connects buyers directly to artists, and has a curation team. Critically: **Artfinder explicitly prohibits AI-generated artwork.** If your product's brief is AI-generated, the platforms most aligned with fine artists are the ones whose rules you'd be arguing with.

### 3. Etsy — the volume leader with the worst reputation in this set
Roughly 10% effective fees — by far the cheapest of the five — and enormous reach. But **1.4★ across 21,264 Trustpilot reviews with 80% one-star** is the worst rating in any competitor set across these four dossiers. Sellers report unexplained permanent suspensions; buyers report failed protection. Cheap distribution, expensive trust.

### 4. Fiverr — the vetting-and-escrow model, stress-tested
Everything your vetting feature promises, Fiverr already does: identity checks, seller levels, ratings, escrow, milestones, revisions. **And its buyers still report losing money to vetted, highly-rated sellers.** At 20% + 5.5%, it also costs more than Etsy and less than Saatchi. **Read Fiverr as the experiment that already ran.**

### 5. TurningArt — the only one that starts with the space
Curates art *to a physical environment*, commissions from affiliate artists, and sells to businesses — the interior-designer and corporate-buyer segment Section 1 identified as the most concentrated buyer. It has been at this since 2012 on **under $3.2M of funding**, and was recapitalized in 2025 `[a recapitalization usually signals a reset rather than growth — UNVERIFIED as to cause]`.

## What does nobody serve well?

**1. Nobody joins the room to the commission.** AR previewers show you *existing* art on your wall. Marketplaces sell *existing* art. Fiverr commissions new work with **no spatial tools at all**. TurningArt starts from the space but works from an existing catalog and sells to businesses. **Nothing takes a photograph of a wall and turns it into a brief for a new painting.** That specific join is empty — and it is your idea's core.

**2. Nobody vets fine artists on anything meaningful to the buyer.** Fiverr vets on ratings and levels (and still gets defrauded). Saatchi and Artfinder vet on curatorial judgment. **Nobody vets on credentials** — and Section 1 flagged that no evidence links a degree to sales, price or appreciation. `[UNVERIFIED: degree-as-quality-signal remains untested, and it is your product's core filter.]`

**3. Nobody has made commissioning cheaper.** Your premise attacks 30–50% gallery commissions. The online alternatives: **Saatchi 40%, Fiverr 25.5% all-in, Etsy ~10%.** Only Etsy meaningfully undercuts galleries, and it does so by being a mass-market handmade bazaar rather than a fine-art channel. Meanwhile art advisors (Section 2) charge **5–20%**. **The cheap middlemen already exist; they just aren't fine-art-specific.**

**4. Nobody makes the size question trustworthy.** Every wall-preview app in this market has scale complaints. For a **custom, non-returnable** work this is not a nuisance — it's the difference between delight and a four-figure dispute.

**5. Nobody serves the interior designer commissioning original work.** 87,100 interior designers (BLS) do size/palette/theme matching daily. TurningArt serves facilities teams with leased catalogs; nothing serves a designer who wants a *new* piece made for a specific room. **This is the most concentrated, highest-intent, already-paying buyer in the space and it is underserved.**

**6. Nobody has solved artist trust around AI.** Artfinder bans AI art. Cara and Artfol are built on AI refusal. Etsy mandates disclosure. **An AI-brief product enters this market with a supply-side trust problem before it has a single customer** — and your supply side is the one with 347,000 people and no money.

### The sharpest wedge

**A commissioning workflow for interior designers and corporate buyers that starts from a measured photograph of the space, produces a written brief the artist owns, and never generates artwork.** It avoids the AI-supply-trust problem (the brief is text, the human paints), serves the buyer segment that already pays for this labor at 5–20%, and occupies the one join — room to commission — that no competitor covers.
