# What Would Have To Be True — DietGroceryApp at $19.99/month or a Cut of Each Order

**Prepared:** September 22, 2026 · Section 2 of this document.

## On the transaction price, since you asked

**Suggested starting point: 2% of the completed cart, or a flat $1.99 per order — whichever is lower — with the app free.**

The arithmetic that sets the ceiling: a household ordering **4 times a month at $150 a cart** spends $600/month online, so **2% = $12/month** — *less* than the $19.99 subscription. **The transaction model only beats the subscription for households with big carts or high order frequency.** `[The $150/$600 figures are illustrative — this dossier never sourced US household grocery spend. Verify before using.]`

Two things that favor the transaction model anyway: **it charges nothing to a user who doesn't order**, which fits a product people abandon (Section 1: tracking consistency falls 68% → 21% by week 12), and **revenue collected outside the app avoids Apple's 15–30% cut** that a subscription pays.

## Group 1 — Things the research already established

| Assumption | Evidence |
|---|---|
| $19.99 is a real price point in this category | MyFitnessPal Premium and Fitia Premium both charge exactly $19.99/month. |
| It is at the **top** of the range | Plan to Eat $5.95, eMeals ~$4.99, Samsung Food $6.99, MealPrepPro $9.99, MacroFactor $11.99. |
| People do pay for nutrition apps at scale | MyFitnessPal earned $310M in 2025; Cal AI made $40M in its first 18 months. |
| Charging is the most complained-about thing in this category | 39% of 479 critical reviews were about pricing, paywalls and billing. |
| The grocery-ordering rail exists | Instacart's Developer Platform opens ~1B products from 85,000 stores to third-party apps. |
| Buyers already order groceries online at scale | 19.5% of US consumers order groceries online at least monthly. |
| The competing human service costs far more | $150/month (coach + shopper) to $1,500+/month (personal chef). |
| A competitor already sells the same bundle at the same price | MyFitnessPal Premium+ advertises "personalized meal plans, integrated grocery delivery" at $19.99/month. |
| Retention will be the binding constraint | Consistent calorie tracking falls from 68% of users in week 1 to 21% by week 12. |

## Group 2 — Things five conversations next week would settle

1. Do they currently pay for **any** nutrition or meal app, and how much?
2. Would they pay $19.99/month on top of the grocery bill — or does it read as a second grocery fee?
3. **Who in the household actually places the order?** The person who cooks is often not the person who shops, and the app assumes they're the same.
4. Do they order **delivery or pickup**? Pickup is cheaper and may not carry the same fees.
5. Do they shop **one store or several** — warehouse club for bulk, supermarket for fresh?
6. How do they handle **substitutions** today, and who decides when an item is out?
7. Do they use **SNAP/EBT**, loyalty cards or coupons? Those constrain what can be charged and where.
8. Does anyone in the household have a **medical diet** with a dietitian already involved?
9. How often do they re-order the same basket versus plan something new?
10. Would they rather pay **per order** than monthly — and at what number does a per-order fee start to annoy them?
11. What made them **quit** the last diet app they used?
12. Is a GLP-1 part of their situation, and has it changed what they want from food?

**Do these as twelve questions in one 20-minute call. The answer to #3 alone could change the product.**

## Group 3 — Things we cannot know yet

| Unknown | Why |
|---|---|
| Whether Instacart or a retailer will pay an affiliate rate at all, and how much | Not published; requires a partnership conversation. |
| Whether MyFitnessPal's Premium+ bundle is any good | Their support page is 403 to research tools; only Apple's listing confirms the feature exists. |
| Real retention for this specific product | No benchmark exists for a nutrition-plus-grocery subscription; the 68%→21% figure is tracking-only. |
| Whether an AI-built cart can hit ~95% line-item accuracy | Untested, and it's where OpenAI's Instant Checkout failed publicly in March 2026. |
| What it costs to acquire a paying user here | Nobody in the category publishes CAC. |
| Whether the Mealime shutdown (Oct 21, 2026) actually converts to paying users | The 5M+ downloads are real; their willingness to pay is not established. |
| Whether allergen exclusion can be made safe enough to promise | Current models produce unsafe answers 5–13% of the time on medical questions. |
| Whether households will accept a subscription **and** delivery fees **and** a service fee | Three charges on one grocery order; no evidence either way. |
