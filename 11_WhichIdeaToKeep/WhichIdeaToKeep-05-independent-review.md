# Independent Review of the Choice

**Prepared:** September 22, 2026 · Section 5 of this document.
A separate agent read all 20 research files across the four project folders, was told the team chose **07_DietGroceryApp**, and was blocked from reading any `WhichIdeaToKeep` file so its judgment came only from the underlying research. No web access.

## Its answer

**Yes — same choice.** Base cases as each model states them: **07 at $30.7M gross ARR**, 08 at $1.77M, 09 at ~$650k/yr cycle-averaged, 10 at $1.05M. **07's conservative case ($7.7M) is more than 4× the best base case of any other idea**, and it is the only one of the four where somebody demonstrably pays today.

## What it disagreed with

**1. The "genuinely unoccupied" claim is overstated by our own files.** `competitors.md` says no app does nutrient-gap → recipes → cart, "the whole thesis of this project and it is genuinely unoccupied." But `claim-test.md`, same folder, records MyFitnessPal **Premium+** shipping personalized meal plans plus integrated grocery delivery at **$19.99/month**. Honest version: *nutrient-gap-driven planning* may be unoccupied; *nutrition app plus grocery delivery* is not.

**2. The GLP-1 cross-check is not independent.** It is labelled "independent" and carries 45% of the base case — but assumption A11 in the same table says GLP-1 users sit *inside* the 72.1M weight-management population, and the check reuses the identical 91% smartphone and 19.5% online-grocery gates. **It is the same funnel on a subset, not corroboration.** (10_Art's designers-versus-artists check, landing within 12%, is two genuinely different populations.)

**3. The free-chatbot threat is undercounted.** It appears as Theme 7 at 7% — but our own annotation on the eMeals review calls it "the competitive threat to the entire category," and 25% of consumers already use AI to build grocery lists. **It is the newest theme in an 18-month window, so its share is a lagging indicator, not a small one.**

**4. The "$150/month human stack" can't be both the bull case and the central problem.** The same file that uses it to justify $19.99 also says: "the gap between what the job is worth ($150+) and what the category has trained users to pay ($11) is the central pricing problem." Consumers price against Plan to Eat at $5.95, not against a personal chef.

**5. Complaint volume mostly measures installed base.** 479 critical reviews is the largest base of any dossier — but all nine apps rate 4.3–4.9★ overall, only ≤3★ were kept, and theme counts came from keyword matching. **High complaint counts in 07 and near-zero in 08/09 largely reflect which categories have consumer apps at all.**

## Where it says the four dossiers are not comparable

- **Different units:** 07 and 09 are consumer subscriptions, 08 is an annual prosumer plan, **10 is a take rate on GMV**. Store-fee assumptions differ too (20% / 15% / ~3% / ~3%), so gross-to-net ratios don't line up.
- **Conservatism was penalized.** 07 used the strict grocery gate and showed the loose one would give $95.9M. 08's SAM rests on an unverified ×3 attempt multiplier *and* an unverified ×40%; 10's on an invented ×40% of designers; 09's on one nonprofit's self-reported user count.
- **Retention is four different variables** — months of tenure, annual renewal, months-paid per cohort, and completion rate. **09 is the only dossier that applies a seasonality haircut to its own headline (4×).** And **no dossier models CAC**, which bites hardest where 07 needs 219,000 gross adds a year.
- **Complaint bases aren't the same kind of evidence.** Only 07's comes from real in-category competitors. 10's are feature analogs, 09 padded 32 reviews with 119 from a news app, 08 used a grants app and a credit app as proxies.
- **"Few complaints" means opposite things** — an opening or an absent market — and our own files say the data can't distinguish them. Scoring complaint volume and "unoccupied space" together pulls in opposite directions.
- **Price was set by us, not derived.** Re-running 07 at the category median ($11.99) gives ~$18.4M — still first, but the 17–30× margin disappears.

## Its closing judgment

"The decision is right, but the margin is smaller than the dossiers print, the 'genuinely unoccupied' framing is overstated by the team's own claim test, and the two things that should govern the build — **churn at a $19.99 price point that already generates 39% of category complaints, and a checkout rail whose own creator pulled back on multi-item carts** — are the two things most likely to have been discounted."

**One point in our favor it thinks we underrated:** retailer-neutrality plus data export is "a position no well-funded competitor can copy, because their owners bought them for the lock-in" — the most defensible strategic argument in any of the four folders, and not a revenue argument.
