## What SportsLayerApp actually is

Five ideas were researched in that document (sports agency CRM, student-athlete mental health, AI athletic-trainer co-pilot, niche-athlete branding, and a "where to watch" guide). **Only Idea 5 is carried through all five research sections** — opportunity scan, complaints, competitors, market sizing, why-now test — so it is the one scored here.

**Idea 5:** a free AI app where a fan asks "How do I watch the Cowboys Sunday?" and gets four answers — which service has the game, whether their current subscriptions already cover it, the cheapest way to add it, and when to cancel so they stop paying after the season.

## Where SportsLayer lands

**Third on the rubric, second on revenue — and those two facts disagree for a reason worth understanding.**

Its own bottom-up model puts base-case revenue at **$21.5M/yr** ($1.86M conservative, $118.8M optimistic), against 07's $30.7M, 08's $1.77M, 09's ~$650k and 10's $1.05M. **So on money it is second, and close.** It scores lower on the rubric because **96% of that revenue is referral fees paid by streaming services, not by the customer** — the rubric asks whether anyone already pays to solve the problem, and here nobody does.

### Three things it has that 07 does not

1. **The complaints match the pitch more exactly than in any of our four dossiers.** Its biggest theme — "I paid, and the game still wasn't there," ~10 of ~39 complaints — *is* the product gap. In 07, the biggest theme (39% pricing) is a reason people churn, not the thing the product fixes.
2. **The unserved question is crisply defined:** "No product answers 'Can I watch this game, with my plan, here, and what should I cancel afterward?'" Google TV covers 3 leagues and 5 providers; ESPN ranks ESPN first; LiveSportsOnTV "covers everything but doesn't know you."
3. **Free removes the hardest objection in 07.** Our biggest risk is asking $19.99/month in a category where 39% of complaints are about being charged. SportsLayer never asks the fan for money.

### Three things that hold it back against 07

1. **No proven willingness to pay, by anyone.** 07 has $310M and $40M revenue examples at the modelled price. SportsLayer has no evidence any streaming service will pay a referral fee at the assumed rate — that is the single load-bearing number in a model where referrals are 92–96% of revenue.
2. **Three incumbents already ship a partial version** — Google TV, Apple's My Sports, ESPN's Where to Watch — and its own why-now test concedes "Google and Amazon have the AI and still ship only partial versions." That is the same distribution problem that sank the VotingApp to a 6.
3. **The binding constraint is data, not AI.** Its verdict: "the binding limits are data (plan level, location, blackouts), reliability, and business incentives." Knowing a fan's Peacock *tier* is not something any public tool exposes — its research looked and couldn't find one.

## Is this comparable to our four?

Mostly yes, and more so than I expected. Same method (population × adoption × price, bottom-up), same refusal to take a share of a market total, same "could not verify" discipline, and the same blocked sources — **Reddit and G2 were unavailable to that research too**, and it says so plainly.

Four places the comparison is uneven:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Complaint base** | Ours: **479 critical reviews** across nine in-category apps. Theirs: **~39 complaints**, but drawn from far larger products (Peacock 3.7M App Store ratings, Paramount+ 3,854 Trustpilot reviews). Different size, similar quality. |
| **Who pays** | 07, 08, 09 and 10 all model the user paying. **SportsLayer models third parties paying** — referral fees, provider licensing, bar listings. Its revenue is not the same kind of number as ours. |
| **Seasonality** | Its model doesn't apply a seasonality haircut, though the product is season-shaped and its own complaint data is built on season-end cancellations. **09_Voting applied that haircut and it cut the headline by 4×.** |
| **Survey extrapolation** | The "painful core" of ~30M rests on a **Gracenote survey with a US sample of ~503 people**, extrapolated to 115M streamers — the document flags this itself as "my own extrapolation." |

## What I would do with this

**It does not displace 07_DietGroceryApp**, but it is a closer second than 08 was, and it is the only one of the five that avoids the "will anyone pay?" problem entirely by not asking.

The cheapest thing that would settle it is already written in its own document: **the 50-question test.** Write 50 real fan questions for one NFL week ("Cowboys Sunday, I have YouTube TV, I'm in Denver"), answer them by hand, then score ChatGPT and Gemini as-is against a rules-based lookup. **If the free chatbots are already ~95% accurate, the idea has no advantage** — and that test costs a weekend, no code.

Run that alongside 07's three kill criteria before committing.
