# The Case Against 07_DietGroceryApp

**Prepared:** September 22, 2026 · Section 4 of this document. Drawn only from the dossier in `07_DietGroceryApp/`.

## 1. The incumbent already sells this bundle at this price

MyFitnessPal **Premium+** advertises "personalized meal plans, integrated grocery delivery" with automatic grocery lists at **$19.99/month** — the same product at the same price, from a company that did **$310M in revenue in 2025**. It also bought the meal-planning app **Intent** (2025), bought **Cal AI** (closed Dec 2025, $40M sales in 18 months), and integrated with **ChatGPT Health** in January 2026. They are consolidating this exact space while you are researching it.

**What you would see in two weeks:** in interviews, people already have MyFitnessPal and can't name a feature it's missing; or your five paid testers finish a week on Premium+ and say they'd keep it.

## 2. The ordering rail is retreating, and the one product that combined this got switched off

**OpenAI scaled back Instant Checkout on March 17, 2026** — wrong product data, merchant onboarding harder than expected, and **no multi-item carts**, which is exactly what a week of groceries is. Roughly a dozen Shopify merchants ever went live. The industry regrouped around "discover in AI, buy on your own site."

Meanwhile **Mealime — the best combined recipes-preferences-grocery app — shuts down October 21, 2026**, killed by Albertsons, which bought it in 2021 and is folding it into its own store app with **no export button**. The one product that did what you want to do was acquired and switched off by the grocery channel you plan to depend on.

**What you would see in two weeks:** Instacart won't quote you affiliate or partner terms on a call; or another retailer-AI checkout retrenchment story lands; or you read the Developer Platform terms and find consumer apps aren't the intended partner.

## 3. This category's users punish being charged, and they leave anyway

**39% of 479 critical reviews in this category are about pricing, paywalls and billing** — the largest complaint theme by far. Your $19.99 sits at the very top of the range: Plan to Eat $5.95, eMeals ~$4.99, Samsung Food $6.99, MealPrepPro $9.99, MacroFactor $11.99. And the ones who do pay leave: **consistent calorie tracking falls from 68% of users in week 1 to 21% by week 12**, and diet-program dropout runs 57% at six months.

Even the price point itself is tainted — **$19.99 is MyFitnessPal's price, and MyFitnessPal's paywall is the single most-complained-about thing in the reviews.**

**What you would see in two weeks:** fewer than 3 of 100 targeted people enter card details on the landing page; or interviewees say some version of "why would I pay $20 when eMeals is $4.99."

## 4. The two features that would differentiate you are the two you cannot safely ship

- **Allergen exclusion:** across LLMs, **5–13% of responses to medical questions are unsafe**, 21.6–43.2% problematic; a study of AI-generated allergy diets found it produced balanced diets but was **unsafe for one allergen**, with over 60% of cited sources wrong or fabricated.
- **Photo-based nutrition:** the same 2025 research that shows models matching self-report accuracy also finds they **systematically underestimate large portions** and vary widely on macros.
- **And the part you don't control at all:** the delivery itself. **Instacart sits at 1.2★ across 12,279 Trustpilot reviews** — substitutions, missing items, wrong prices, refused refunds. Every order you place inherits that experience under your brand.

**What you would see in two weeks:** in the 20-cart test, more than one line-item error per 20 lines after two rounds of fixes; or one allergen appearing in the 20 allergy plans; or the first real test orders arriving with substitutions the user rejects.
