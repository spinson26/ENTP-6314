# HushPlate survey results — summary

**Date:** 16 Oct 2026
**Source:** `research/1. Qualtrics - Survey Results - (10.14.2026).csv` — 19 completed responses, collected 9–12 Oct 2026, anonymous link. All 19 finished the survey (100% progress).

**Read this first:** 19 people is a small sample, and it was shared by link, so it is probably friends, classmates and their networks rather than a random group of longevity eaters. Treat the numbers below as direction, not proof. Percentages are out of 19 unless stated.

---

## 1. Top features (Q1, pick all that apply)

| Rank | Feature | People | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Daily nutritional requirements output | 17 | 89% |
| 2 | Preferred foods list | 12 | 63% |
| 3 | Diet choice selection | 11 | 58% |
| 4 (tie) | Health statistics input | 10 | 53% |
| 4 (tie) | Healthier restaurant choices (3 options from a menu) | 10 | 53% |
| 4 (tie) | Workout education | 10 | 53% |
| 4 (tie) | Workout tracking and motivation | 10 | 53% |
| 8 (tie) | Recipe selection on a calendar | 9 | 47% |
| 8 (tie) | Grocery consumption learning (sales, promos) | 9 | 47% |
| 10 | Excluded foods list | 8 | 42% |
| 11 (tie) | InBody scan input | 7 | 37% |
| 11 (tie) | Delivery options (curbside or home) | 7 | 37% |
| 11 (tie) | Restaurant eating (app picks exactly what to eat) | 7 | 37% |
| 14 (tie) | AI agent ordering (AI fills the cart) | 6 | 32% |
| 14 (tie) | Restaurant consumption learning (CheapFoodMap, under $10) | 6 | 32% |
| 14 (tie) | Gamification | 6 | 32% |
| 17 | Daily motivation quotes | 5 | 26% |

**What it means**

- The number people want most is the plain one: "tell me what I need to eat each day." Nearly everyone picked it.
- The **AI agent filling the grocery cart** — the feature the grocery side of HushPlate is built around — is near the bottom at 6 of 19. This matches the 6 Oct interview finding that grocery ordering was not a strong pain.
- On eating out, the "give me 3 healthier options" version (10) beat the "pick exactly what I eat" version (7). People seem to want guidance with a choice, not orders.
- Gamification and daily quotes are the least wanted.
- Two people picked every feature; two people picked only one.

**Features people asked for that weren't listed (Q2):**

- Portion sizes for each meal, and planning for a family of 3–5 rather than one person.
- Alerts about local food contamination at specific stores (e.g. salmonella recalls).
- Healthy drink suggestions.
- Daily calorie counts for weight loss.

## 3. Grocery ordering and delivery fees (Q6–Q10)

**How they'd rather pay for grocery ordering:**

| Option | People |
|---|---|
| Flat fee per delivery | 9 (47%) |
| Percentage of the order | 7 (37%) |
| Monthly subscription for unlimited delivery | 3 (16%) |

**Flat fee per delivery — most they'd pay (9 people):**

| Fee | People |
|---|---|
| $0 | 1 |
| $1–$4.99 | 2 |
| $5–$9.99 | 5 |
| $10–$19.99 | 1 |

**Free deliveries wanted each month before a fee starts (same 9 people):** 1 free (3 people), 2 free (1), 3 free (2), 4 or more free (3).

**Percentage of the order — highest they'd accept (7 people):** 3% (3 people), 4% (1), 5% (2), 6% (1). Middle answer: **4%**.

**Monthly unlimited delivery — most they'd pay (3 people):** $5–$9.99 (1), $20–$24.99 (2).

**What it means**

- The most common answer is **a flat $5–$9.99 per delivery**, with a few free deliveries each month thrown in.
- People who prefer a percentage want it small — 3–6%. For comparison, on a $100 order 4% is $4.
- Almost nobody wants a delivery subscription.

## 6. Why they don't use an app like this now (Q13)

| Reason | People |
|---|---|
| Tedious logging (weighing, scanning, searching) | 10 (53%) |
| Cluttered interface (pop-ups, ads, paywalls) | 10 (53%) |
| Never thought they needed an app | 9 (47%) |
| Cost | 7 (37%) |
| Messy real life (restaurant and shared meals hard to log) | 6 (32%) |
| Privacy — don't trust how apps handle health data | 5 (26%) |
| Fading motivation (just numbers, no long-term insight) | 4 (21%) |
| Judgmental design (streak resets, red warnings) | 2 |
| Anxiety and food guilt from calorie counting | 2 |
| Didn't know these apps existed | 1 |
| Other (typed in) | 2 |

The two "Other" answers: "Really not interested in this type of app" and "I don't like people picking out my food."

**What it means**

- The top two complaints — tedious logging and clutter — are problems HushPlate can design around: less typing, no ads, a clean screen.
- **9 of 19 never thought they needed an app.** That's the biggest warning sign here. These people don't feel the problem yet, so selling to them will be hard.
- 5 people raised privacy. With HushPlate collecting health data and GLP-1 dosage, a clear and simple privacy promise matters. *(Compliance flag: this is the health-data concern CLAUDE.md asks to review.)*
- Restaurant meals being hard to log (6 people) supports the restaurant menu features.

---

## Mixed signals worth noting

- One person picked 12 features but also wrote "Really not interested in this type of app." Picking features in a survey doesn't mean someone will pay.
- One person picked 16 of the 17 features but also said "I don't like people picking out my food." That's a warning for the "app picks exactly what you eat" idea.

## Bottom line

1. **Lead with daily nutrition targets, food preferences and diet choice** — the most wanted features.
2. **The AI cart is not what draws people in.** Present it as an extra, not the headline.
3. **Price around $10–$15 a month, or under $120 a year**, with a **30-day free trial**.
4. **Delivery: a flat $5–$9.99 per order**, with 1–3 free deliveries a month.
5. **Grow through word of mouth and Instagram**; consider partnering with trainers and gyms.
6. **No ads, no clutter, little logging** — these answer the top reasons people don't use apps like this today.

## What this survey could not tell us

- Whether these 19 people are actually longevity eaters, GLP-1 users or dieters — the survey didn't ask about age, goals or current habits.
- Whether anyone would really pay. These are stated prices, which usually run higher than what people actually pay.
- Results by group (for example, whether yearly payers want different features). 19 responses is too few to split.
