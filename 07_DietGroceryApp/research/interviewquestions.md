# Customer Discovery Interview Guide: Dieters

**Prepared:** 2026-10-02 · **Project:** 07_DietGroceryApp · **Length:** 20 minutes · **Method:** The Mom Test ([Rob Fitzpatrick](https://www.momtestbook.com/))

**Goal:** learn how people who are trying to lose weight, including people on GLP-1 medications, plan, shop for and cook their food today. Learn where it breaks down, what that costs them, and what they already pay for. The interview is not for testing the app idea.

**Rules for the interviewer**

- **Don't pitch.** Don't describe the app, show the landing page or say "AI agent." If they ask what you're building, say "I'll tell you at the end. I don't want to steer your answers." Then tell them only after the last question.
- **Ask about the past, not the future.** Use "last time," "last week" and "walk me through." Don't ask "would you," "could you see yourself" or "how much would you pay."
- **Get specifics.** When they say "usually" or "always," ask about the last time it actually happened.
- **Compliments and opinions aren't data.** "That sounds great" means nothing. Steer back to what they did.
- **Talk less than 20% of the time.** Let silences run. Most of the useful detail comes after a pause.

**Who to interview (screen before booking):** adults who have actively tried to lose weight in the last 6 months. Aim for about half on a GLP-1 (Wegovy, Zepbound, Ozempic, Mounjaro or a compounded version) and half not. Don't recruit people who know about the app idea.

**Note on question tags:** a question marked **[Leading, see L#]** was flagged in the self-review at the end of this document. Ask the rewrite from the review table instead.

## Opening (1 minute)

Read this out, close to word for word:

> Thanks for making time. I'm researching how people who are trying to lose weight handle food day to day: deciding what to eat, shopping, cooking. I'm not selling anything, and there are no right answers. I'll mostly ask about things that already happened, like last week. Is it OK if I take notes?

## 1. Context (2 minutes)

- **Q1.** Tell me where things stand with your weight and your eating right now.
- **Q2.** How long have you been working on it? What has changed in that time?
- **Q3.** Is anyone else involved, like a doctor, dietitian, coach, partner or family member you cook for?
- **Q4.** Are you taking any medication for weight loss right now? If yes: when did you start, and what changed about how you eat?

## 2. Current workflow (4 minutes)

- **Q5.** Walk me through last week. How did you decide what you were going to eat?
- **Q6.** Where did that food come from: a store, grocery delivery, takeout, a meal kit? Who bought it, and how?
- **Q7.** Do you keep track of what you eat? If yes: can you show me what you use? (An app, a notebook, photos. Ask them to open it.)
- **Q8.** Where did the last recipe you cooked come from? **[Leading, see L1]**
- **Q9.** About how much time did planning and shopping take you last week? **[Leading, see L2]**
- **Q10.** Which part of that takes the most effort? **[Leading, see L3]**

## 3. The last time it went wrong (4 minutes)

- **Q11.** Tell me about the last time your eating plan went off track. What happened? **[Leading, see L4]**
- **Q12.** When was that? What day, what time, and where were you?
- **Q13.** What did you end up eating, and how did you get it?
- **Q14.** What did you do afterward, that day or the next?
- **Q15.** Tell me about the last time you got home from shopping, or a delivery arrived, and something you needed for your plan was missing. **[Leading, see L5]**

## 4. What it cost them (3 minutes)

- **Q16.** What did that last time cost you: money, time, progress on your weight? **[Leading, see L6]**
- **Q17.** How much did you spend on food last week across groceries, delivery and takeout? Can you check? (A banking app, receipts or order history.)
- **Q18.** How much food did you throw out last week? **[Leading, see L7]**
- **Q19.** Have you ever stopped a diet because the planning and shopping got to be too much? **[Leading, see L8]**

## 5. What they've already tried (3 minutes)

- **Q20.** What have you tried in the last couple of years to help with eating for weight loss? Apps, programs, meal kits, coaches, anything.
- **Q21.** Which of those did you stop using? When, and what was going on when you stopped?
- **Q22.** Which are you still using? What has kept you using it?
- **Q23.** Have you gone looking for something to fix this? What did you search for, and what did you find? **[Leading, see L9]**

## 6. What they pay for today (2 minutes)

- **Q24.** What do you pay for today that's connected to food or weight loss? Subscriptions, delivery memberships, meal kits, a coach, medication you pay for yourself.
- **Q25.** Can you pull up your subscriptions or recent charges so we can go through them? (Phone settings, app store or bank.)
- **Q26.** Who pays for each one: you, insurance, an employer, someone else?
- **Q27.** What's the last food or diet thing you canceled, and why? **[Leading, see L10]**

## Close (1 minute)

- **Q28.** Is there anything about how you handle food that I should have asked about and didn't?
- **Q29.** Who else do you know who's trying to lose weight that I should talk to? Would you be willing to introduce me?
- **Q30.** Can I follow up with you in a few weeks?

If they asked what you're building, tell them now, in one or two sentences, and write down their reaction separately from the interview notes.

## What to listen for

| Strong signal | Weak signal |
|---|---|
| They describe a specific recent time, with a day and details | "I usually...", "I always...", "I'd probably..." |
| They have already spent money or time on a fix (an app, a meal kit, a coach) | They say it's annoying but have never tried anything |
| They built their own workaround (a spreadsheet, a fixed weekly menu, a standing order) | Compliments: "That's a great idea" |
| They open their phone and show you | They talk in general terms and can't find an example |
| They offer an introduction or ask to be followed up with | They're polite but have no next step |

## Notes template (fill in within an hour of the call)

- **Who:** age range, GLP-1 yes or no, who they cook for
- **Current workflow:** how they decide, shop, track
- **Last time it went wrong:** the date and what happened
- **Cost:** money, time, progress, food thrown out (numbers they checked, not guesses)
- **Already tried:** what they tried, what they quit, why
- **Pays for today:** each item, the amount, and who pays
- **Best quote:** their words, written down at the time
- **Surprises:** anything that contradicts what you expected
- **Next step:** introduction, follow-up or nothing

## Self-review: leading questions in this guide

I reviewed every question against two tests. Does it tell the person what answer is expected? Does it assume the problem the app solves, which is that planning, counting and shopping are a burden and that grocery runs go wrong? Ten of the 30 questions failed one of these tests. They're tagged in the guide and listed below with a rewrite to use instead.

| Flag | Question | Why it leads | Ask this instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Q8. Where did the last recipe you cooked come from? | It assumes they cook from recipes. People who eat the same few meals, or don't cook, may make up an answer. | Tell me about the last meal you made at home. How did you decide on it? |
| L2 | Q9. How much time did planning and shopping take you last week? | It assumes they plan. It also names "planning" as a task, which plants the app's framing. | What did you do last week to get food into the house? Walk me through it. |
| L3 | Q10. Which part of that takes the most effort? | It assumes something takes effort and invites them to rank a pain they may not feel. | Of everything you just described, which part did you do last? What happened? (Then listen for whether effort comes up unprompted.) |
| L4 | Q11. Tell me about the last time your eating plan went off track. | It assumes they have a plan and that it goes off track. It also suggests "off track" is the expected story. | Tell me about the last day your eating didn't go the way you meant it to, if there was one. |
| L5 | Q15. The last time something you needed for your plan was missing after shopping or delivery. | This is the most leading question in the guide. It describes the exact failure the AI ordering agent fixes and assumes it happens. | Tell me about your last grocery order or shopping trip. What did you get, and how did it go? (Let them bring up missing items on their own.) |
| L6 | Q16. What did that cost you: money, time, progress on your weight? | Listing the three costs tells them which answers count, and "progress on your weight" suggests the cost should be large. | What happened as a result of that? (Then follow up on whatever they name.) |
| L7 | Q18. How much food did you throw out last week? | It assumes they waste food, and people under-report waste when asked this way. | What happened to the food you bought last week? Did all of it get eaten? |
| L8 | Q19. Have you ever stopped a diet because the planning and shopping got too much? | It's a yes/no question that hands them the app's thesis as the reason. A polite person will say yes. | Tell me about the last time you stopped a diet or eating plan. What was going on? |
| L9 | Q23. Have you gone looking for something to fix this? | "This" means the problem as I framed it in earlier questions, not as they described it. | Have you searched for any app, product or service related to food or weight loss in the last few months? What were you looking for? |
| L10 | Q27. What's the last food or diet thing you canceled, and why? | It assumes they canceled something. The effect is mild, but people may invent an answer. | Have you canceled or stopped any of those in the last year? If yes: which one, and what was going on? |

**Questions reviewed and kept as written:**

- **Q4 (GLP-1 use)** asks about a fact. Ask it after Q1 to Q3 so it doesn't feel like the point of the call. If they don't want to answer, move on.
- **Q5, Q6, Q7, Q12 to Q14, Q17, Q20 to Q22, Q24 to Q26** ask about specific past behavior or ask them to show something, and they don't suggest an answer.
- **Q28 to Q30** are standard Mom Test closing questions that ask for a commitment (an introduction or a follow-up), not for an opinion of the idea.

**Pitch check:** no question names the app, AI, an agent, automatic ordering or a price. The opening line names the topic (weight loss and food) but not a solution. The rule to hold the explanation until the end keeps the interviewer from pitching if the person asks.
