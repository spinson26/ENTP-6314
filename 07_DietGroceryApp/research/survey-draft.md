# Survey Draft: Diet Planning and Grocery Behavior

**Prepared:** 2026-10-06 · **Project:** 07_DietGroceryApp (HushPlate) · **Length:** 12 questions, about 5 minutes · **Format:** online survey (Google Forms, Typeform or similar)

**Goal:** find out whether people already do the things this app would automate. The app's seven features plus gamification are mapped to questions in the table below.

**The rules every question must follow:**

1. No leading questions.
2. No double-barreled questions (two questions in one).
3. No questions about hypothetical future behavior. Ask only about what people have actually done.

**A limit to keep in mind:** a survey that follows rule 3 can't ask "would you use this app?" It can only show whether people already do each step by hand. Someone who already tracks their macros, plans the week's meals and orders groceries online is a likely user. Someone who does none of those is not.

**Don't describe the app anywhere in the survey**, including the title, intro and thank-you page. If people know what's being tested, they'll tilt their answers toward it. Use a neutral title like "How people plan, shop for and eat their food."

## Which questions test which feature

### Violations

**Q10 is double-barreled.** "Delivery or pickup" are two different behaviors. Someone who uses curbside pickup every week and never uses delivery answers the same as someone who uses delivery every week. Feature 7 is about *delivery*, so the answer can't be read. In the interviews, I3, I4 and I7 used pickup or curbside and nobody used delivery. That split is exactly what this question would hide.

**Q12 is leading, because it assumes something that may not be true.** "Think of the last app… that had streaks, badges, points or challenges" assumes the person has used one, and there's no "I haven't" option. People who never used a gamified app will either skip the question or pick an answer that isn't true. The question also only asks about people who *did* use one, so it can't show how common gamified apps are.

### Borderline

**Q5 is borderline double-barreled.** "Calorie, protein, carb or fat target" lists four different targets. Read as "any nutrition target," it works as one question, but someone who tracks protein and not calories may not know how to answer. Fix: ask about "a daily nutrition target of any kind." I first tried moving the "which target" detail into Q6, but that made Q6 ask two things (which targets and which tool), so it was dropped to keep the survey at 12 questions with no violations. If you need to know which targets people track, add it as a separate 13th question.

**Q6 is borderline leading.** "The last time you checked" assumes the person has checked. There is an "I haven't done this" option, but it comes last, after the question has already implied that checking is normal. Fix: show Q6 only to people who chose 1 or more in Q5 (skip logic), and drop the "haven't" option.

### Clarity problems (not rule violations)

- **Q2:** some people won't know what "BMR (basal metabolic rate)" means. Add "your daily calorie needs at rest."
- **Q3:** the brief lists "suppressed-appetite GLP-1 user" as a diet. It's a medication situation, not a diet, so it was left out of Q3. GLP-1 use is covered in Q1.
- **Q7:** "a medical reason" leaves out people who avoid foods for religious, ethical or diet reasons (in the interviews, I10 buys kosher meat and I20 is vegetarian). Feature 5 covers allergens *and* excluded foods. Fix: change the reason to "for any reason other than not liking the taste."
- **Q8:** "plan" is vague. Does it mean the meal was decided, or the ingredients were bought? Fix: use "decided what it would be."

## How to read the results

Each question shows whether people already do one of the app's steps by hand. The more steps a respondent already does, the more likely they are to use an app that automates them.

| Feature | Real-demand signal | Weak-demand signal |
|---|---|---|
| (1) Health stats | Q2: records weight or BMR | Q2: none of these |
| (2) Choose a diet | Q3: followed a named diet | Q3: none of these |
| (3) Nutrition check | Q5: 11 or more days | Q5: 0 days |
| (4) Preferred foods | Q4: 3 or more repeat dinners | Q4: 0 to 2 |
| (5) Excluded foods | Q7: avoids at least one food | Q7: none |
| (6) Recipe calendar | Q8: 3 or more dinners decided ahead | Q8: 0 dinners |
| (7) Grocery delivery | Q10: at least one online delivery in the last 3 trips | Q10: all in-store |
| Willingness to pay | Q11: $1 or more on delivery or app subscriptions | Q11: $0 on both |
| Gamification | Q12: used one for 1 month or more | Q12: haven't used one, or less than a week |

**What to look for:** count how many respondents meet the real-demand signal on Q5, Q8 **and** Q10 together. That group already tracks, plans and orders online, and it's the closest the survey can get to "people who would use this app." In the interviews, no one fit that description: nobody regularly tracked in an app, and nobody used grocery delivery.
