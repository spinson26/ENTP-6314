# Verdict: Weighing the Case Against and the Case For

**Prepared:** 2026-10-06 · **Project:** 07_DietGroceryApp (HushPlate) · **Weighs:** the two sections above · **Evidence:** the same 20 interviews (IDs I1–I20), nothing else

## The decision

**Don't build HushPlate as it's pitched today. Don't drop the problem either.** Run one more round, aimed at a narrower group of users, with a hand-run test instead of software.

The case against wins on the product as described: a grocery-ordering agent for dieters who juggle apps. The case for wins on the problem underneath it: busy people run out of energy to plan and fall back on fast food. Twenty interviews, about 8 of them with the wrong people, can't settle whether a narrower version of the product fixes that problem.

**Score: Against 5, For 2, 1 tie, 1 unresolved.**

## Why the case against wins on the current product

1. **The timing problem is the deciding issue.** HushPlate's job ends when the grocery cart is approved. Most failure days happen later, at the moment of cooking or eating: fatigue (I14, I18), cravings (I8, I9), illness (I16) and schedules (I12, I17). The case for's best rebuttal still leaves 63% of failure days untouched.
2. **There's no evidence anyone would pay.** The case for shows people spend money *near* this problem, not *on* it. Osta failing at $3/month is the closest comparison in the data, and it failed.
3. **The landing-page pitch isn't supported.** No interviewee described "juggling a tracker, a recipe app, and a grocery app." Whatever happens next, that sentence should go.

## What the case for gets right and shouldn't be thrown away

1. **Running out of time and energy and grabbing fast food is the most common pain (12 of 20).** I16 traced it directly to "not having energy to meal plan." That's a real problem with a real cost.
2. **There's a user that fits:** busy people who cook for a household and plan when they have time. I1, I3, I4, I7 and I16 fit most of it. Three of them have young children or a household to feed, and three use pickup or curbside.
3. **Targets matter to 8 of 20, and allergies and restrictions came up without being asked** (I13, I15, I16, I20). The profile part of HushPlate is aimed correctly. It just isn't the part people would pay for.

## What would settle it

Neither case can be settled with this sample. About 8 of 20 weren't dieting, only 2 are on a GLP-1, and there's no measured spending. Round 2 should fix those gaps and test the narrow version.

**Who to recruit (12–15 people):**

- Tried to lose weight in the last 6 months, with at least 5 on a GLP-1.
- Does most of the shopping and cooking for a household of 2 or more.
- Got takeout or fast food at least twice last week.

**Method:**

1. Run the interview guide again with the fixes from the synthesis, and actually ask Q17 and Q20–Q26.
2. Then offer a **hand-run test**. For two weeks, we build each person's weekly plan and a pickup cart at their store by hand. No app.
3. Ask for a small commitment up front, such as a refundable deposit or a booked planning session, rather than a yes to a survey.

**Go criteria. Build the narrow version if all three hold:**

1. At least half describe a recent takeout day caused by having no plan or no groceries, without being prompted.
2. At least 60% of the hand-run test users actually place the pickup order we built, in **both** weeks.
3. At least a third make a paid or refundable commitment.

**Kill criteria. Stop work on the planning-and-cart idea if any of these hold:**

1. Hand-run test users skip their order in week 2.
2. Failure stories are still mostly about after the food was home: cravings, fatigue at cooking time, illness.
3. Nobody commits money.

If it's killed, the fallback the evidence points to is the moment of cooking, not shopping. People already pay for premade and pre-marinated food to save effort (I7, I8, I13, I19). A plan built around quick or premade meals is the next idea to test.

## Bottom line

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Build HushPlate as pitched?** | No. |
| **Is the problem real?** | Yes. Busy people run out of energy to plan and grab fast food (12 of 20). |
| **What's the narrow bet?** | A household meal plan that holds one person's targets, turned into a pickup cart, for busy cooks who already use pickup or curbside. |
| **What's next?** | Round 2 with the right people, a two-week hand-run test, and a money commitment. Go or kill based on the criteria above. |
| **What changes now?** | Drop "juggling a tracker, a recipe app, and a grocery app" from the landing page. Lead with the time and energy problem instead. |
