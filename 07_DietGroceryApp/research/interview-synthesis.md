# Customer Interview Synthesis: Dieters (Round 1)

**Prepared:** 2026-10-06 · **Project:** 07_DietGroceryApp (HushPlate) · **Source:** "3. List of Questions to Ask Customer - (10.5.2026) - COMBINED.docx", 20 interviews · **Guide used:** [interviewquestions.md](interviewquestions.md)

**The idea being tested:** HushPlate pulls nutrition targets, recipes and a meal plan into one place, then an AI agent builds the week's grocery order at the user's store for them to approve. The landing page's stated pain: "juggling a tracker, a recipe app, and a grocery app that don't talk to each other."

**Bottom line:** These 20 interviews do not support the core pain the landing page claims. None of the 20 regularly tracks food in an app today (5 used one in the past and stopped or dropped to the free tier), nobody uses a grocery delivery service, and none had searched for a meal-planning or grocery tool. The pains that came up most often were running out of time or energy and falling back on fast food (12 of 20), and knowing what to eat but not sticking to it (9 of 20). People describe the second one as a discipline problem, not a planning problem. Three said outright that they won't pay for a food app, and two more cancelled paid ones.

## 5. What to ask better next time

**Fix the process**

1. **Recruit the right people.** About 8 of 20 weren't trying to lose weight and only 2 were on a GLP-1. Screen before booking: "In the last 6 months, have you done anything specific to lose weight? What?" Then fill a GLP-1 quota.
2. **Ask the questions that were skipped.** Q12–Q14 (when, what was eaten, what happened after), Q17 (last week's spending, checked in a banking app) and Q20–Q26 (what they've tried and pay for) aren't in the record. Without Q17 and Q24–Q26 there is no spending data at all, and Q27 ("any of those") made no sense to people.
3. **Write down exact words, not summaries.** Several answers read like notes. Record or type exact phrasing, especially for anything emotional.
4. **Actually ask to see the app.** Q7 said to have them open what they use. Nothing shows that happened. Seeing someone's MyFitnessPal history or notes-app list is worth more than what they say about it.
5. **Write down each person's reaction to the pitch.** The guide says to note it separately, but no reactions are in the file. If there are notes, add them.

**Fix the questions that confused people**

6. **Q10 confused people.** I12: "I don't understand how to answer." I16: "Unsure what the question is asking." Ask instead: "Last week, was there a step in getting food on the table that you put off or skipped? What happened?"
7. **Q16 was answered about the shopping trip, not the cost of things going wrong.** Tie it to the Q11 story: "That day you ended up getting fast food: what did it cost, and how did you feel about it the next day?"
8. **Don't follow up with leading questions.** "Do you think price is a problem?" (asked of I2) prompted "I am willing to pay for an app." Ask instead: "What do you pay for today that's related to food or weight?"

**New questions to test where the idea is weakest**

9. **Delivery and curbside.** "When did you last use curbside or delivery? What made you use it that time? What stopped you the other times?" The agent produces an online cart, so we need to know if anyone shops online at all.
10. **Who decides and who shops.** "Who decided what was for dinner last night? Who bought the food for it?" I9, I15 and I20 don't shop for themselves.
11. **Why plans fall apart.** "Tell me about the last time you had a plan and the food in the house and still didn't follow it. What time was it, and what was in the fridge?" This tests whether a ready-made plan plus groceries would have changed anything (pains 1 and 2).
12. **What they pay for convenience.** "What did you buy last week that was premade, pre-marinated or frozen? What did that cost compared with making it yourself?" This was the strongest spending signal in the round.
13. **What interviewees said we should ask:** what time they eat (I12), late-night snacking (I8), allergies, intolerances and dietary restrictions (I13, I15, I16, I20, who is vegetarian), health conditions and medication side effects (I16), whether they like cooking (I13), and "What has the biggest influence on what you eat day to day" (I17).
14. **Ask for a real commitment at the end.** All 20 said yes to a follow-up survey. That costs nothing and doesn't tell us much. Ask for something that costs them: joining a waitlist with their email, booking a 15-minute session to plan their next week, or a refundable pre-order.
