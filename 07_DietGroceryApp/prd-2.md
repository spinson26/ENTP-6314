PRODUCT: HushPlate



USER: A longevity eater above the age of 18 located in the United States (including dieters, GLP-1 users, and people who want to eat healthy food daily to either lose weight, maintain weight, gain muscle, and/or prolong their life through healthy eating choices), who wants an app that will curate healthy eating choices via recipes which they choose, and then an AI agent fills the users grocery cart with food for those recipes, which the user will approve the final purchase of the food from the grocery cart, or the longevity eater can choose a healthy food choice from a restaurant on the days the user does not want to cook food, and the app will aggregate the information for the longevity eater. 



PROBLEM: People eat unhealthy because they do not know their daily nutritional requirements, they lack the time to curate recipes, they lack the time to make grocery lists according to those nutritional requirements and recipes, they lack the time to go to the grocery store to pick up the food to make at home, and they lack the know-how to determine whether their daily eating choices are diminishing their longevity or lifespan according to what they are eating daily, and they lack statistics to show their doctor.



CORE USER STORY:

As a longevity eater (including dieters, GLP-1 users, and people who want to eat healthy daily to either lose weight, maintain weight, or gain muscle), the user can plug in their health statistical information into the app (height, weight, BMI, BMR, muscle mass, visceral fat, GLP-1 dosage, or Inbody Scan Statistical information) so that the app will solve the following issues in the users daily life to save them time:

(1) Diet Goals (lose weight, maintain weight, gain muscle)

(2) Diet choice selection (Longevity, Mediterranean, DASH, Flexitarian, Ketogenic, Atkins, Low-Carb, Vegan, Vegetarian, Pescatarian, Carnivore, Intermittent Fasting, Daniel Fast, Paleo, Low-FODMAP, GLP-1 suppressed appetite)

(3) Daily nutritional requirements output (calories, fat, protein, carbs, vitamins, minerals, water consumption)

(4) Preferred foods list (foods the user likes to eat)

(5) Excluded foods list (foods the user dislikes, that cause problems, or that the user is allergic to)

(6) Pantry Feature (a user can input what foods that it has in their pantry, so that recipes and a grocery cart can be aggregated, and used to pick recipes and/or add or take food out of the grocery cart) 

(7) Recipe aggregation and selection (recipes chosen on a daily, weekly or monthly calendar based on the diet, nutritional requirements, and preferred and excluded foods list)

(8) AI agent ordering (the AI fills the users grocery cart at a local store and the user decides whether to place the order)

(9) Delivery options (curbside pickup or home delivery)

(10) When to eat (the app will let the user know when to eat the appropriate food amounts throughout the day according to the recipes selected by the user, with the last meal of the day eaten 3 hours before bed)

(11) Restaurant eating: the app reads a menu (online or from a photo) and picks exactly what to eat to meet the user’s nutrition targets

(12) Healthier restaurant choices: the app reads a menu and gives the user 3 healthier options that meet the user’s nutrition targets

(13) Grocery consumption learning: over time the app learns what the user eats and recommends recipes, foods and grocery sales, discounts and promos

(14) Restaurant consumption learning: over time the app learns what the user eats and recommends restaurants, deals and meals under $10 (via CheapFoodMap app)

(15) Workout education (app educates the user on how to work out)

(16) Workout tracking and motivation (app tracks a users daily workouts and motivation via user confirming what they current plan to do each day, and then at end of day, there is an End of Day Daily Confirmation - #21 below)

(17) Workout plan (app will allow the user to input their daily workouts that they plan to do each day, when inputting their health statistics into the app)

(18) Gamification (rewards that keep the user using the app daily, weekly, monthly)

(19) Daily motivation (positive quotes to start or support the user’s day)

(20) Access to a longevity doctor (app has the ability to connect a user to a longevity doctor for consultation by providing contact information)

(21) End of Day Daily Confirmation Audit (the app will confirm via a yes/no answer that the user cooked and ate their food that day, or ate at a restaurant that day, and confirm the user worked out for the day, and the app will provide inputs where the user can change what happened that day, this will allow the app to learn; when a user changes what happens at the end of day, the user can change food amounts, or workout details; the user can change which foods or restaurant meals they had, not just amounts.)

(22) App Recommendations (the app will provide recommendations to all users based on their history of using the app (as well as the history of top 10% of users using the app that confirm their grocery orders and workouts during the End of Day Daily Confirmation Audit) for the attributes listed above - #1 to #19) 

(23) User Health Report (a user who would like to speak or visit with a longevity doctor, may export their data via the app through User Health Report; the User Health Report, will contain all the health metrics of the patient, specifically, the health statistical information inputted into the app by the user (height, weight, BMI, BMR, muscle mass, visceral fat, GLP-1 dosage, or Inbody Scan Statistical information)  and items #1 to #7, and #10 to #12, #15 to #17, and #21)

(24) Family and Friends (the app allow the user to create a family group and/or friend group for accountability, where the user can share their Daily Health Score)



The app is used to save the user time by curating all these attributes for the user so the user can obtain the proper foods for them on a daily basis, track their food and workouts, and be able to access a longevity doctor to help them with their yearly longevity eating plans. 



The app improves over time, and for the user over time. The app improves over time by aggregating the days data from day to day for each user, providing daily recommendations. As more people join, the app will improve by providing updates regarding popular choices for top 10% of users on the app, providing weekly recommendations to all users. A popular choice is what the top 10% of users average choices are using the app based on their End of Day Daily Confirmation Audit. For average choices of the Top 10% of users, that are not numbers, the average would be the amount of times that option was chosen by the Top 10% of users. Top 10% users are ranked by how often they confirm results for all items in the End of Day Daily Confirmation Audit for the entire week by answering “yes”. Users who skip the End of Day Daily Confirmation Audit one (or more times) throughout the week do not count for the Top 10% users for the week. If Top 10% users are tied, then the users avatar name will break the tie by ranking in alphabetical order. If fewer than 10% of users confirm during the week, then continue to rank the remaining users based on how often they confirm results for all items in the End of Day Daily Confirmation Audit for the entire week by answering “yes”. If fewer than 10% of users skip the End of Day Daily Confirmation Audit one (or more times) throughout the week do not count for the fewer than Top 10% users for the week. If the fewer than Top 10% users are tied, then the users avatar name will break the tie by ranking in alphabetical order. If a user answers “no” and enter changes to the End of Day Daily Confirmation Audit, they can still be considered a Top 10% user, or fewer than Top 10% user, as long as they complete the End of Day Daily Confirmation Audit everyday for the week. If a user answers “no” and enters changes to the End of Day Daily Confirmation Audit, they are scored in a similar manner as the Top 10% of users, or the fewer than Top 10% user, and a corrected day counts less than a “yes” day by 25% less. 



The app will also provide a user a Daily Health Score, that will be aggregated on the app based on their End of Daily Confirmation Audit. The Daily Health Score contains several metrics to average the users health score according: (1) the amount of food eaten compared to daily recipe meal chosen, or restaurant meal chosen (#7 or #11 in CORE USER Story) (scored out of 50), (2) the amount of workout time actually worked out compared to the daily workout plan chosen (#17 in CORE USER Story) (scored out of 30), and (3) whether a user ate their last meal 3 hours before bed time (#10 in CORE USER Story) (scored out of 20). The health score can be used by the user to track daily, weekly, monthly goals based how well the user has been able to stick to using the app. The app will give a user a Daily Health Score everyday after the End of Day Audit. The user can then compare that score with other users of the app, either against the Top 10% of users, or family and/or friends also using the app who the user shares the data with in a family and/or friend group. The Daily Health Score aggregates the data on a daily, weekly, and monthly basis. Scoring for the Daily Health Score, is given by partial credit based on the confirmations by the user saying “yes” if they kept to their daily plan. For scoring the workout points, each cardio session, and workout session earns a share of the 30 points.

When to eat, the app tells the user when to eat based on spaced eating habits, with meals spaced at intervals of a meal every 3 hours, for 6 total meals a day for the users daily nutritional requirements. The meals are broken down into 3 meals, and 3 snacks per day: meal 1, snack 1, meal 2, snack 2, meal 3, snack 3. The user must have their last meal 3 hours before bedtime for the Daily Health Score. During the onboarding process, the user inputs their expected daily wake times, and bed times. During the End of Day Audit, the user can change their wake time and bedtime, and confirms their last meal of the day. For a user to know when to eat, the user is told when to eat via an alert. For those users, who choose to eat more meals than 6 meals per day (3 meals and 3 snacks), their meals are to be spaced evenly throughout the day based on their choice. For those users, who choose to eat less than 6 meals per day (3 meals and 3 snacks) their meals are to be spaced evenly throughout the day. 



For users that are in GLP-1 user or intermittent fasting user, during onboarding the user can include the amount of meals and snacks the user plans to eat per day. The default amount of meals is 3 meals, and 0 snacks for a GLP-1 user or intermittent fasting user. This type of user can change this via the app at any time to recalibrate the app. 



The grocery stores the app depends upon are agnostic. We plan to use agnostic AI agents who have access to a multitude of grocery stores to order food from. The AI agent will: (1) puts together a grocery cart, (2) will take payments, and (3) read CheapFoodMap to make recommendations to the user.





As for the doctor, the app will connect a user to a doctor via the app by giving the user the ability to set up an appointment with the doctor, where the app only gives contact information of the doctor and not any data. Any data provided to the doctor comes directly from the user. The doctor can be booked Monday through Friday, 8:00 to 5:00 PM, Central Time, throughout the year. The doctor will be a longevity doctor, whose contact information will be listed on the app (the contact information will be provided later). The user will pay that doctor separately for services. The doctor will not have access to any information on the app; however, the user can export the data and provide the data to the doctor, or the user can just hand over the data to the doctor at the doctor’s visit by showing the doctor their app. 



As for CheapFoodMap, which is a third-party app, the AI agent will read that CheapFoodMap recommendation of food under $10 and make recommendations to the user. 



As for the daily workout plan, the app will contain inputs for the user to select whether they did some form of cardiovascular work (e.g., walked, ran, sprinted, biked, cycled, hiked, swam, elliptical, stair stepped, rowed, kickboxed, high intensity interval training, core training, yoga, pilates, danced, tai chi), or worked out using free weights. Each of these workouts track the period of time and calories burned for that period of time based on the users health statistics, and what the user inputted as part of their daily workout plan. And for the workout time, the user will set the workout time for the app by inputting the number of time they plan to spend on that activity as part of the initial survey of health statistics. The app will ask the length of time for the cardiovascular work, and the free weight work.



As for the User Health Report, material outside the User Health Report is kept by the app (orders, menu photos, rewards, recommendations).



As for Avatar Names, the user is provided a random avatar name when using the app. The user has the ability to change their avatar name but the user cannot make their avatar name there actual name, or any name that would identify them as a user while using the app. The app will prevent a user from using an avatar name that matches their first or last name, or truncated words of their first or last name, or other identifying names that connect back to the users first name or last name with 2 letters in sequential order matching the first name, the last name, or the first and last name.
